Sorted roughly by how much of a jaw-drop it was at the time. Every card links out to the primary source, so you never have to take my word for any of it.

01 Science

Worked out the shape of 200 million proteins

A protein's shape is what decides what it does. Pinning down a single one used to eat months or years of lab time — people built whole careers on one structure. AlphaFold predicted nearly every protein we know about and put the entire database online, free, for anyone who wants it.

Nature AlphaFold DB
02 Medicine

Found a new antibiotic for one of the nastiest hospital bugs

Acinetobacter baumannii is the sort of infection that turns up in hospitals and shrugs off most of what we throw at it. AI screening pulled out abaucin — a narrow-spectrum candidate that goes after that one bug instead of flattening everything else living in you.

Nature Chemical Biology
03 Medicine

Turned up an antibiotic that kills in a way we hadn't seen before

MIT researchers pointed a model at an enormous pile of molecules and it flagged halicin, which works by a mechanism unlike the antibiotics already on the shelf. That last part is the whole point: "unlike the existing ones" is exactly what drug-resistant bacteria haven't learned to handle yet.

Cell
04 Medicine

Read mammograms with fewer misses and fewer false alarms

Reading mammograms is hard, and the two ways of getting it wrong pull against each other — flag too much and you terrify healthy people, flag too little and you miss cancers. A Google Health system beat expert radiologists on both counts at once, which is the part that surprised people.

Nature
05 Medicine

Screening for the eye disease that quietly blinds diabetics

Diabetic retinopathy doesn't hurt until it's already done damage, so people skip the appointment. The FDA has authorised AI that reads retinal photos and flags it, which means the check can happen at a normal clinic visit instead of a specialist referral nobody gets around to booking.

FDA authorisation (PDF)
06 Medicine

Got stroke patients in front of the right team in minutes

With a large-vessel stroke you're losing brain tissue the whole time you sit waiting for someone to look at your scan. Software now spots those scans and pulls the team in within minutes — and with strokes, minutes are the currency.

Viz.ai
07 Medicine

Cut radiotherapy planning from hours down to minutes

Before radiation treatment, somebody has to outline the tumour and every organ near it, slice by careful slice, so the beam hits the right thing and skips everything else. It's painstaking work that used to take hours. AI does the first pass in minutes and the clinician checks it over.

Nature Communications Multicentre trial
08 Planet

Beat the old weather models at their own job

GraphCast forecasts global weather more accurately than many traditional systems, across hundreds of variables. Weather prediction has been a physics-and-supercomputers problem since the 1950s, so this one landed hard with the people who do it for a living.

Science
09 Planet

Flood warnings in more than 80 countries

Google Research's flood forecasting now covers over 80 countries and reaches millions of people. River gauge networks are expensive and plenty of places don't have one, so a model that works where the sensors aren't is the difference between a warning and no warning at all.

Google Research
10 Tech

Laid out chip floorplans that would've taken engineers months

Deciding where every block sits on a chip is a maddening puzzle, because moving one thing changes everything else. AI produced floorplans that human engineers would have spent months on — and those chips go on to run everything else in this list.

Nature
11 Planet

Knocked about 40% off data centre cooling

DeepMind's system took over the cooling controls at Google's data centres and cut that energy use by roughly 40%. Same buildings, same servers, considerably less power — which is a rare thing to be able to say about anything.

DeepMind
12 Science

Put two million new crystal structures on the table

Materials science has always moved at the speed of somebody having a hunch about what to try next. DeepMind's GNoME predicted over two million candidate inorganic crystals in one go. Not all of them will pan out, but that's a very different starting point than a hunch.

Nature
13 Planet

Spotted wildfires earlier, from orbit

AI reads satellite imagery to catch fires while they're still small. Anyone who's lived through a fire season knows the entire game is the first hour — after that you're not fighting the fire so much as following it around.

Google Blog FireSat
14 Medicine

Gave people with paralysis a voice again

Researchers decoded brain signals into speech, close to real time, for people who had lost the ability to talk. Not a menu of canned phrases — actual sentences, as they try to say them. If you only read one paper off this page, make it this one.

Nature
15 Tech

Made translation good enough to actually hand someone your phone

Neural translation went from something you'd screenshot and post for laughs to something you'd genuinely trust in a conversation with a stranger. It's probably the AI accomplishment the most people use, and the one they think about least.

Google Translate
16 Tech

Drove tens of millions of miles with nobody in the driver's seat

Waymo publishes its crash numbers, which is already more than most of the industry bothers with. Across tens of millions of rider-only miles, the cars are in far fewer injury-causing crashes than human drivers on the same roads. Whatever you make of robotaxis, that figure is on the record and it updates as the miles pile up.

Waymo Safety Impact
17 Access

Started describing the world out loud, on demand

Be My Eyes used to mean a blind person ringing a sighted volunteer and hoping somebody picked up. Now you point the phone at a menu, a thermostat, a letter from the council, and get a detailed description back in seconds — free, no waiting, and without owing anyone a favour for it.

Already in your pocket

You use this stuff every day

None of this gets called AI anymore. It just gets called "my email" or "my maps app," which is usually how you can tell something worked.

Technology stops being impressive at roughly the moment it becomes useful. Then it's just the thing that's always been there.

  • 01

    Spam filters bin billions of junk emails a day. You never see them, which is the entire job.

  • 02

    Fraud detection catches a stolen card mid-swipe, while the transaction is still in the air.

  • 03

    Your maps app reroutes you around traffic that hasn't reached you yet.

  • 04

    Captions, live transcripts and image descriptions — the difference between usable and not, for a lot of people.

  • 05

    Radiologists get a second read on scans, on every scan, without waiting for a colleague to be free.
